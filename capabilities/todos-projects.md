# ✅ To-Dos & Projects
> One trusted list instead of three apps and a worried feeling. Big projects
> broken down until the next action takes 30 minutes or less.

**Serves pillars:** Home & Logistics (mostly), all indirectly
**Personal file:** `systems/todos.md`

## What your assistant does
- Keeps a single list with three horizons: **Now** (this week), **Next** (this
  month), **Someday** (parked, guilt-free).
- Triage happens *for* you: inbox items land in the right horizon during the
  weekly review, duplicates merged, stale items challenged.
- Never hands back a raw list. The weekly review names **your 3 for this week**
  — chosen for deadlines, energy, and what's blocking other things.
- Breaks projects ("redo the garage") into steps until the **next action is
  ≤30 minutes**, and only ever surfaces that next action.
- Keeps a **15-minute wins** shelf — small tasks pre-matched to a surprise free
  evening or a kid's nap.
- Balances load between adults if you want it to, and calls out when one person
  is quietly carrying the list.
- Celebrates completions in the review — one line, genuinely.

## Setup questions
Batch 1:
1. Where do your to-dos live today? (head / notes app / paper / partner's
   memory) — anything you want to paste in right now to import?
2. Realistic hours per week for house projects and errands, honestly.
3. The top 3 things that have been nagging you for months.

Batch 2:
4. Should I track whose task is whose, or keep one shared pool?
5. Any recurring chores worth putting on autopilot reminders (trash night,
   plant watering, car service)?

## Files
- `systems/todos.md` — Now / Next / Someday, projects with next actions,
  15-minute wins shelf, recurring chores.

## Cadences installed
- Weekly triage — inside the weekly review.
- Someday sweep — monthly: revive, retire, or re-park each item.
- Any recurring chores from setup — at their stated rhythm.

## Commands
- `/capture ‹task›` → lands in the inbox like everything else.
- "what should I do tonight?" → one pick from the 15-minute wins shelf, matched
  to the time you have.

## The world-class bar
- The family trusts the list enough to stop keeping backup lists in their heads.
- Projects move because next actions are small; nothing "stalls at step zero."
- The Someday list is a parking lot, not a graveyard — everything gets a
  monthly heartbeat.
